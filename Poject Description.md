# DESCRIPTION

Our project is called STARBASE, a fictional space company that investigates and observes stars. Our idea for this project was to have a way to collect data and showcase it in an unconventional way through a game.
As a new employee, your job is done all in the office. At the end of every day, you are given data from the astronauts, and you have to figure out which star systems they went to. You are given the distance in light years from earth, the magnitude of their brightness and which constellation they are part of. Using the information you’re given, you need to go through the STARDEX to find which stars match the information that the astronaut gives you. 
Our project had a few inspirations. The visual and aesthetic inspiration comes from the game Lethal Company, where we wanted to create a terminal like the one in the game:

<img width="825" height="464" alt="image1" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/092c05bb-4e94-476f-8ef3-2b58e6ee1f3a" />

We actually opened the game and played around to get more ideas from it. Initially, we also wanted to have some of the same mechanics, like a scanning function. In lethal company, you can scan your surroundings, and you'll receive data that tells you what you're looking at. We wanted to create something similar where you could scan whatever star you're looking at, and it will give you information on it, but not all the information is available. But we felt that would be a really hard mechanic and too much for our project, so we kept the information part, where you're given a dataset, and some of the information in the dataset is redacted. 

Another point of inspiration came from the game Krillion, where you are asked questions and have to give answers. The more niche your answer is, the better. Obviously we did not take the same mechanic, but the question and answer format is what inspired us for the input aspect of our project. 

We also talked through a lot of different iterations of the game. At first we created a whole story behind STARBASE to give it a little more life, but with time constraints and for the sake of simplicity, we just went with a terminal program. At first, we wanted to use both the space catalog API, as well as the NASA images API. Together, when a user searches for a specific star, we want to have an image of that star displayed on the page from the NASA images API, and have both of them communicate to display something on the page. But with the terminal idea, we felt that that didn't really fit with the idea. 

Overall, we had a lot of fun creating this project because we went with a theme that unconventionally reflected the reading in a way that isn't necessarily positive or negative. We usually tend to see data retention and usage in a bad way, so it was our way of showing how you can use data in a way that isn't bad.
